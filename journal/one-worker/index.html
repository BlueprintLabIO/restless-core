One strong worker is the default inside one outcome.

Finding 01Coordination economicsBounded experimental result

When work is tightly coupled, adding autonomous minds can make the result slower and worse. The new workers need briefs. They form partial views of the same problem. Someone must reconcile their output, repair mismatches and reread the assembled whole.

Across current Restless experiments, those costs often exceeded the extra cognition. Decomposing a task was necessary, but it was not enough. A seam had to produce independently valuable evidence or stable expertise.

Team size is not the unit of ambition. A coherent outcome is.

What the runs showed

Two-operator-plus-assembler sales work and three-worker research arrangements lost on complete-outcome economics. The extra structure created more coordination without a proportionate improvement in the final artifact.

One sequential marketing chain did win blind quality. Two specialists passed the work in sequence, and each contribution had a distinct purpose. That counterexample matters. The finding is not “solo agents always win.”

The product decision

Restless now prefers a wide, shallow organisation: several accountable leads can run in parallel, each with one end-to-end Staff worker by default. This keeps portfolio concurrency without slicing every outcome into a miniature bureaucracy.

A lead can add Staff when specialisation, independent evidence, locally closing repeated units, unused capacity or context saturation is likely to repay the boundary. That remains a judgement call, not a team-size formula.

What remains unknown

We do not yet have a reliable observed signal for the moment one coherent outcome exceeds a strong worker’s effective session. The marketing win also needs replication outside that workload. Until then, one worker is a prior that should be challenged by evidence.

Independent units behave differently.
