# Restless evidence pack

These are concise, bounded findings from the current coordination programme. Preserve qualifications.
The public site may edit and visualise them, but it may not turn them into universal claims.

## Finding 1: one strong worker is the default inside one outcome

On tightly coupled work, extra autonomous minds often lost to one strong end-to-end worker because
briefing, mutual-state maintenance, integration and rereview cost more than the additional cognition.
Decomposability was necessary but not sufficient. A seam had to produce independently valuable,
differentiated evidence or expertise.

One sequential two-specialist marketing chain did win blind quality, while two-operator-plus-assembler
sales and three-worker research arrangements lost on complete-outcome economics. This supports a
wide, shallow organisation with one worker per lead by default, not a universal solo-agent rule.

## Finding 2: repeated independent units are a different scaling regime

When 240 sales accounts were simultaneously available, two workers crossed the throughput threshold
twice. When the same population arrived gradually, extra workers did not improve p90 response time.
Four workers were faster in one arm but weakened marginal tail quality.

An independent monitoring workload also crossed at 40 entities: two workers delivered about 1.9 times
throughput and 0.55 times p90 latency, with lower charged cost per alert. Worst-unit consistency fell
by 1.5 points. Demand shape and local closure mattered more than department name or total task count.

## Finding 3: supervision must remain available, but presence is not correctness

In a support experiment, causal policy delivery prevented 16 stale outcomes and closed 96 of 96
cases. A terminal-only comparison accepted 80 of 96. The same supervised run also introduced one
duplicate-case contamination that independent evaluation had to catch.

The owner has therefore kept the lead as a distinct non-producing mission keeper and final judge,
even when it adds latency. The system still needs independent outcome checks at consequential
acceptance boundaries.

## Finding 4: completion is an event and artifact fact, not a timer

Fixed-timeout retries destroyed near-finished work in an earlier run. Current theory treats elapsed
time as a safety envelope and uses provider completion, process exit, Work and Attempt state, artifact
existence and material callbacks as semantic signals. Polling may observe but should not define
completion.

## Finding 5: sparse factual structure beats mechanical coordination

Natural lead judgement above a minimal factual substrate produced better work than forced handoff
rituals in one controlled screen. Work and Attempt records were still essential because they exposed
a claimed Staff contribution that never happened. The enduring pattern is intelligent free-form
leadership with attributable responsibility, not structure-free collaboration or a rigid workflow.

## Finding 6: the first site comparison was inconclusive

The first same-model Restless-versus-Codex website run began from the same mature incumbent. Both final
candidates passed the objective browser matrix and blind reviewers split, with mean scores differing
by less than half a point. Codex closed materially faster; Restless produced a richer interactive
product demonstration and required fewer repair loops. Both inherited nearly the same visual identity.

That run measured coordination on an existing codebase. It did not provide a useful test of independent
creative direction, which is why this greenfield run exists.

## Open questions worth publishing

- What observed signal predicts that a coherent outcome exceeds one strong worker's effective session?
- When does a bounded parallel hand stop being a tool and become a Staff responsibility?
- Which native artifact and event boundaries generalise from code to research, documents and live
  operations?
- Can durable dispatch return Exec availability before lead orientation completes?
- When do two separately accountable leads outperform one broader lead without turning Exec into a
  relay?
- Can a company react to inbound real-world signals while preserving authority, idempotency and owner
  attention?
