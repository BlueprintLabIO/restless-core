# EXP-17 findings

## Enduring conclusion

Coordination is justified by a coordination problem, not by task size alone. A fast capable model can
hold a coherent bounded artifact in one context, so adding Exec and lead model turns creates fixed
translation, orientation and verification cost. That cost pays only when the separate role changes an
outcome: authority, exception repair, concurrent capacity, continuity beyond the worker's durable state,
or a decision the worker cannot safely make alone.

EXP-17 found none of those crossovers in its four valid first pairs. This is a routing result, not a
claim that teams are useless.

## What changed in the theory

1. **A durable worker absorbs much of the old manager value.** The solo Codex thread survived a forced
   process death, contradictory evidence, a duplicate signal and scheduled follow-up. Work/Attempt plus
   a lead did not improve that outcome in this sample.
2. **A supervisor role can remain necessary without requiring a model turn on every task.** Authority
   and accountability are distinct value. Routine commissioning and acceptance narration are not.
   Keep the lead as the accountable control point, but wake its intelligence on material signals,
   policy ambiguity, failed gates, lineage conflicts or owner-facing judgement.
3. **The Work graph survives as a sparse control plane.** Keep ownership, dependencies, attempts,
   causal events, effects, gates and immutable receipts. Do not use the graph as a mechanical model of
   thought or decompose one coherent artifact merely to make the organisation visible.
4. **Parallel work remains a separate hypothesis.** One worker completing 24 independent account units
   does not test capacity scaling. `RP-Q2` needs disjoint input custody, locally closing gates, direct
   delivery and no generic synthesis. It must beat the best single-worker arm by at least 30% elapsed
   time or throughput at quality parity.
5. **Hidden evaluators must test owner semantics.** Exact strings, data shapes or acceptance conventions
   are valid only when the starting contract makes them observable. An undocumented preference is an
   evaluator bug even when frozen before execution.
6. **Review custody is part of correctness.** An immutable artifact that the reviewer cannot read is not
   blind evidence. Candidate packaging must normalize read-only permissions and prove access before
   paid review begins.
7. **Concurrency needs accounting identity.** Independent model sessions are insufficient when their
   spend settles into one actor bucket. Each concurrent stream needs an atomic request/attempt identity,
   or the company must serialize charged turns.

## Human-team analogy

The conclusion generalises to people with one important difference in scale. Human teams pay much
higher communication latency but have long-lived local knowledge, social trust and genuine concurrent
attention. They outperform one person when the work exceeds one mind's attention horizon, requires
different authority or expertise, or contains many independently closing responsibilities. They lose
when managers repeatedly translate a task one capable person can already own.

LLMs move the crossover outward for coherent work because token generation and durable context are fast.
They move it inward for broad independent queues because identical workers can be copied cheaply. The
best architecture is therefore asymmetric: one coherent owner per artifact, many owners for locally
closing units, and sparse supervisors for mission, authority and exceptions.

## Limits

This is one valid pair per work shape, not a statistical population. Tasks were bounded, simulated and
free of live external effects. The LC replay changed only a defective evaluator but is still a second
stochastic sample. No `RP`, transfer, multi-department or authority-sensitive live workflow ran here.
Those regions remain empirical questions.
