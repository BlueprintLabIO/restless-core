# Your first useful outcome

Prepare a client proposal, review it, and ask for a revision. This example gives
Restless all the business facts it needs, so you can judge its work without first
connecting email, researching a market or sharing customer data.

After [starting Restless](../README.md#getting-started), open **Company →
Intelligence** and connect your chosen agent or API provider. Return to the main
workspace and send this to Exec:

> We're a small design consultancy preparing a proposal for a fictional client,
> Northstar Books. They need a clearer homepage and a reusable newsletter template.
> Our fixed project price is $2,400, delivery is two weeks after approval, and the
> price includes two rounds of revisions. Hosting, copywriting and ongoing support
> are excluded. Draft a concise client proposal as a native shared document with
> the scope, schedule, price, exclusions and a clear next step. Use only these
> facts; flag any missing detail rather than inventing it. Delegate the production
> work, check the result, and bring the document back here for my review. Don't
> send or publish anything.

You should receive an actual proposal you can open and edit. Work and People show
who owns the result while it is being prepared. You shouldn't need to repeatedly
ask Exec whether it has finished.

Read the proposal as if you were sending it to a client. Check that the price,
schedule and exclusions match the brief. Add a comment on the payment terms, then
send this revision request to Exec, including the document's title:

> Update the proposal with 50% due on approval and 50% on delivery. Keep the fixed
> price, two-week schedule and exclusions unchanged. Make the next step a short
> approval checklist. Save the changes in the same document and tell me when I can
> review them. Don't send it to anyone.

Open the document again and inspect the changes. Its history should preserve the
earlier draft. The useful result is the proposal itself, with your judgement and
the AI's supporting work brought together.

Then try the same loop with a real task: replace the example facts with your own
brief and decide what a good output would look like before delegating it.
