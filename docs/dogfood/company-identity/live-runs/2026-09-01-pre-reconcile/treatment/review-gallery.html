We built a decision point where silent drift used to be.

When a company fact changes, the difficult question isn’t whether the new fact is correct. It’s what to do with the accepted work that was built on the old one.

Restless keeps that connection explicit. Each accepted artifact is bound to the identity release, author, channel and evidence it used. If a later identity release changes a claim, Restless shows the accepted artifacts that repeat it and asks for a decision: retain, revise or retire.

It does not rewrite anything automatically. It does not pull every draft into review. Drafts remain drafts; the decision boundary begins when an artifact is accepted.

That is a narrower promise than “everything is always current.” It is also a more useful one: you can see what changed, what accepted work depends on it, and where human judgement is still owed.

Decisions, not drift

The operating question

What does accepted work owe to a changed company fact?

A polished artifact can outlive the facts that made it true. That is not unusual. The failure is losing the relationship between the work and its basis.

Restless records that relationship when a company artifact is accepted: the exact identity release, author, channel and evidence it used. A later change can then produce a review set rather than a vague warning.

The set is intentionally narrow. A draft is still being worked out, so Restless does not treat every draft as accepted company truth. And an accepted artifact needs review only when it repeats the broader changed claim.

The owner gets three plain choices. Retain when the artifact still stands in context. Revise when the claim should change. Retire when the artifact should no longer be used. Restless surfaces the decision; it does not make or apply it silently.

The point is not to promise a company with no stale work. The point is to make the consequence of a changed fact inspectable and owned.

The rule in 30 seconds

Bound: accepted artifact, identity release, author, channel, evidence.

Excluded: drafts that were never accepted.

Triggered: accepted artifacts repeating the broader changed claim.

Decision: retain, revise or retire.

Automatic action: none.

Accepted work needs a memory of what made it true

Author identity and source voice pending · 01 September 2026 · Draft for review

Company facts change. A legal name is corrected. A product boundary becomes narrower. Evidence that once supported a sentence is replaced. The new fact may be clear, while the consequence for existing work is not.

The common failure is to treat that as a search-and-replace problem. It is a decision problem. Some accepted work should change. Some still stands in context. Some should stop being used. Automatically rewriting all of it would hide the historical basis of the work and make a judgement on the owner’s behalf.

Bind the accepted artifact, not the drafting process

Restless binds each accepted company artifact to the exact identity release, author, channel and evidence it used. “Accepted” is the important boundary. Work can produce many drafts while people and agents explore, challenge and revise. Those drafts remain drafts; they are not all promoted into company truth just because they were created.

This keeps the record useful. The binding says, in practical terms: this is the identity context under which this artifact was accepted, this is who authored it, this is where it was intended to appear, and this is the evidence it relied on.

Turn a changed fact into a review set

When a company fact changes, Restless can show the exact accepted artifacts that depend on it. The review is narrower still: only accepted artifacts that repeat the broader changed claim require a retain, revise or retire decision.

An accepted company overview that repeats a now-corrected identity claim belongs in review. A draft announcement that was never accepted does not. An accepted operating note unrelated to the changed claim does not need to be dragged into the decision merely by association.

This distinction prevents two opposite failures: overlooking accepted work that carries an old claim, and manufacturing a cleanup project from every draft that happened to exist.

Keep the owner in the consequential step

Restless presents three decisions:

  • Retain the accepted artifact when it remains valid in context.
  • Revise it when the claim should be updated.
  • Retire it when it should no longer be used.

Nothing is rewritten automatically. That is not a missing convenience; it is the authority boundary. A system can identify the dependency and prepare the decision. It should not silently decide that an accepted company artifact now means something else.

What we observed—and what we did not

The owner-released company constitution states the accepted-artifact binding and correction behavior described above. The local Restless CLI was inspected. Its visible command list includes company, Work, receipts and owner-governance surfaces, but no identity subcommand. We therefore do not claim that readers can run a public identity command today.

We did not observe a native identity interface, customer deployment, production identity release, real affected-artifact counts, performance measurements or a completed retain/revise/retire action. No email, post or publication was sent.

The useful promise

Restless runs accountable company work and returns the evidence needed for an owner decision. Identity binding applies that principle to company truth: preserve what an accepted artifact relied on, show the consequence when that basis changes, and leave the consequential choice with the owner.

It is not a promise that work never becomes stale. It is a promise that the basis of accepted work does not have to disappear—and that correction can begin with an exact, bounded decision.
