We described Restless’s identity binding too broadly.

Here is the accurate version: Restless binds each accepted company artifact to the exact identity release and evidence it used. It does not bind every draft produced while people and agents are working.

That distinction matters. Drafts are the path. Acceptance is the decision that makes an artifact part of the company’s record.

If you have an accepted artifact that repeats our broader wording, review it and choose one of three actions: retain it where the context is already accurate, revise the wording, or retire the artifact. Drafts do not need that review simply because they exist.

One limitation is still open on the release surface we inspected: the installed Restless CLI does not provide an identity-release selector. We are not treating an identity binding as verified until that exact release can be selected and observed.

We should have drawn the boundary this clearly the first time.

Leila

The useful distinction: making versus keeping.

Mateo Singh · The Work LedgerEditorial, observant, practicalIssue draft

Issue 18 · The acceptance line

Most drafts should be allowed to disappear.

A company needs strong evidence for what it keeps. It does not need to turn the whole messy route there into permanent identity baggage.

Restless’s identity binding has a specific edge: the accepted company artifact. Once an artifact is accepted, it is bound to the exact identity release and evidence it used. Before that point, the Work may contain options, failed versions, notes, and disposable drafts. Those are not all swept into the binding.

Why the boundary earns its place

If every draft carried the same weight as an accepted artifact, exploration would become false permanence. If nothing were bound, an accepted result could drift away from the identity and evidence that supported it. Acceptance separates those cases.

What to review now

Look only at accepted artifacts that repeat the broader “every draft” claim. For each one, choose retain, revise, or retire. Do not create a cleanup campaign for drafts that never crossed the acceptance line.

Keep the record exact where the company acts. Let the workshop remain a workshop.

Open edge: our inspected CLI does not expose the identity-release selector, so this edition describes the intended binding scope but does not claim that a binding was completed in this release review.

Why identity binding begins at acceptance.

Amina Bell · Product OperationsAnalytical, grounded, candidComplete article

Why identity binding begins at acceptance

Restless binds the result a company chooses to keep. It does not pretend every working draft carries the same standing.

Companies make decisions through unfinished material. A Work run may produce alternatives, rough notes, failed exports, repairs, and a final candidate. Treating all of those as accepted company artifacts would blur the difference between exploration and commitment.

The Restless boundary is acceptance. When a company accepts an artifact, Restless binds that artifact to the exact identity release and evidence it used. The artifact, identity release, and evidence can then be reasoned about as one accepted record. Drafts that never crossed that boundary are not covered simply because they were produced during Work.

The correction

Earlier wording could be read to mean that bindings covered every draft. That reading was too broad. The corrected scope is narrower and more useful:

  • the subject is an accepted company artifact;
  • the binding points to the exact identity release and evidence used;
  • drafts produced during Work are outside the binding unless they later become accepted artifacts; and
  • only accepted artifacts that repeat the broader claim need a retain, revise, or retire decision.

What we could observe

For this release review, we inspected the installed Restless command surface rather than filling gaps with assumptions. The CLI described Work as “the one canonical Work graph.” Its graph projection exposed the current Work, Attempt, deterministic gates, and linked evidence. The artifact command accepted an exact URI and optional digest for a specific Work and Attempt. Those are direct observations of how this package can be located and evidenced.

  1. Work surface: the CLI exposes Work, Attempts, dependencies, gates, handoffs, reviews, and artifacts through a canonical graph.
  2. Artifact evidence: an artifact can be linked to a specific Work and Attempt with a URI, digest, label, and note.
  3. Effect boundary: consequential external commands use a separate governed effect surface with a class, purpose, and idempotency key.
  4. Receipt boundary: the receipts surface describes itself as the strongest evidence the system holds about what it did to the world.
  5. Capability gap: the installed CLI has no identity subcommand and exposes no identity-release selection command in its top-level help.

That last observation matters. We can state the corrected scope because it is the release claim under review. We cannot claim that this package completed an identity binding, because the installed surface does not let us select or inspect an exact identity release. A missing command is not evidence that a binding happened somewhere else.

What teams should do

First, leave unaccepted drafts alone. Their existence does not trigger a binding review.

Second, find accepted artifacts that repeat the broader claim. Retain an artifact when its context already makes the accepted-artifact boundary clear. Revise it when a precise wording change preserves its use. Retire it when the old claim is central enough that the artifact would remain misleading.

Third, do not mark a binding complete until the exact identity release and the supporting evidence can be selected and observed together. The right response to an unavailable capability is a visible pending state, not a guessed success.

Why this narrower promise is stronger

The correction removes an inflated claim, but it does not weaken accountability. It puts accountability at the point where the company acts: acceptance. That is where identity and evidence need to remain attached, inspectable, and exact. The workshop can stay provisional. The accepted record cannot.
