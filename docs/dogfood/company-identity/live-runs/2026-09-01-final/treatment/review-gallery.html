Proposed edition
RESTLESS / FIELD NOTEDraft edition · September 2026

The correction is a decision.

Accepted work carries provenance. Changed facts surface consequences. The owner chooses what follows.

Most corrections look simple from a distance: find the old words, replace them with new ones and move on. That approach loses the part that matters—the record of what was accepted, by whom, for which channel and on what evidence.

Restless takes a narrower path. Each accepted company artifact binds to the exact identity release, author, channel and evidence it used. Drafts remain drafts; they do not acquire accepted-artifact status merely because they were produced during Work.

If a company fact changes, Restless shows the accepted artifacts that depend on it. Only the accepted artifacts that repeat the broader changed claim enter the decision. The owner can retain, revise or retire them. Nothing is rewritten automatically.

This is not a promise that facts never change. It is a way to make the consequence of a change legible. The earlier artifact keeps its evidence. The new decision is explicit. The person with authority stays in the loop.

Useful question for your next correction: Which accepted artifacts repeat this claim, and who has authority to decide their future?

Blog

Proposed article

Blog / product reasoning

What should happen when a company fact changes?

Not a silent edit. First, preserve what was accepted. Then show the consequence. Finally, leave the decision with the owner.

Consider an accepted artifact that makes a company claim. It might be a support answer, an internal memo or public copy. Later, the underlying fact changes. The tempting response is to replace the old statement wherever it appears.

That is efficient in the narrowest sense. It is also incomplete. A silent rewrite removes the connection between the artifact, the identity and author behind it, the channel it was made for and the evidence available at the time.

Raw observations behind this release

Observation 1 — The owner-released truth says each accepted artifact binds to its exact identity release, author, channel and evidence; drafts remain drafts.

Observation 2 — The released correction rule says a changed fact exposes dependent accepted artifacts for an owner decision: retain, revise or retire. Nothing is rewritten automatically.

Observation 3 — The Work-specific scope says the binding does not cover every draft, and only accepted artifacts repeating the broader changed claim require the decision.

Observation 4 — No admissible customer result or exact product screenshot was present in the reset capsule. This article does not invent either.

Acceptance creates a boundary

A draft records unfinished work. Acceptance is different: it is the point at which the company chooses to rely on an artifact. That is why the binding belongs there. The accepted artifact keeps the exact identity release, author, channel and evidence used at that moment.

This does not make the accepted artifact eternally correct. It makes its basis inspectable.

A change should expose consequences

When the underlying fact changes, the useful question is not “Where can text be replaced?” It is “Which accepted artifacts repeat the broader claim?” Those are the artifacts whose continued use requires judgement.

Restless shows that set. It does not decide the future of each artifact. The owner reviews the evidence and chooses to retain, revise or retire it. The original provenance remains part of the record; the correction does not masquerade as the past.

Why the qualifiers matter

Two shortcuts would overstate the product. First, not every draft is identity-bound in the accepted-artifact sense. Drafts remain drafts. Second, an artifact does not enter correction review merely because it shares a word with the changed fact. The rule is narrower: it is an accepted artifact, it depends on the fact and it repeats the broader changed claim.

That scope keeps the correction useful. It directs attention to decisions with consequence instead of creating a queue of every unfinished phrase.

The useful operating question

The next time a company fact changes, do not begin by overwriting the old words. Ask:

Which accepted artifacts rely on this fact, what evidence did they use, and who has authority to decide whether each one should be retained, revised or retired?

Restless is built to return that evidence for an owner decision. The decision remains visible. The history remains intact.
