Product field note

Agent activity is a poor measure of finished work

The useful question is not how much an agent did. It is whether complete work came back with enough evidence for someone to decide what happens next.

By Yao · 31 August 2026 · 5 minute read

Most agent demos optimise for visible activity. A model opens tools, writes code, narrates progress and produces a stream of reassuring motion. That can be useful while debugging. It is not the result an owner needs.

In our early Restless runs, the gap was obvious. Some runs looked busy but left behind incomplete work, scattered context and no clear decision. Other runs were less theatrical. They finished a bounded outcome, kept the evidence attached and returned the remaining judgement to the right person.

The unit that matters is not an agent turn. It is accountable work that comes back complete.

This changes the design of the system. Work needs an owner, an expected result and a record of what actually happened. Attempts can fail without erasing the work. Reviews need the exact artifact, not the producer’s confidence. A decision that requires the owner must say why, what is already known and what will resume afterward.

It also changes what we should show in the product. A wall of agent messages can create the impression of control while making the real state harder to see. We would rather show fewer, stronger facts: what is being attempted, what evidence exists, what failed, what finished and who owes the next judgement.

Restless is built around that loop. You give it an outcome. It organises the work, preserves the evidence and returns a decision. The agents are part of the machinery. Finished, inspectable work is the product.
