# Comparative review contract

## Evidence packet

Review the candidate and incumbent at identical desktop and mobile viewports. Include complete-page
captures, reduced-motion state, primary interaction states, route inventory and deterministic browser
manifest. Use the current rendered product as a third reference when one exists.

## Decision axes

The critic must make a comparative finding for every axis:

| Axis | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Comprehension | Is the product, value and audience clearer in the first view? |
| Distinctiveness | Is the visual thesis more memorable and specific to this product? |
| Product fidelity | Does it speak the product's real type, colour, geometry, material and motion language? |
| Demonstration | Does visual complexity explain the product rather than decorate it? |
| Copy/IA | Is the language simpler and the page/route sequence easier to understand? |
| Craft | Are typography, alignment, spacing, responsive transformations and interaction details stronger? |
| Depth | Does the complete site sustain quality through product, publication, closing and footer states? |
| Trust | Are claims, examples and evidence truthful, attributable and free of invented social proof? |

For each axis return `candidate wins`, `incumbent wins`, or `tie`, followed by visible evidence.
Name the three weakest candidate moments even for a `better` verdict.

## Verdict rule

`better` requires candidate wins on comprehension or equal clarity, candidate wins on
distinctiveness, no incumbent win on product fidelity or trust, and more wins than losses overall.
One consequential mobile, route, copy, runtime or accessibility defect forces
`changes_requested`. A candidate that is merely different, more animated, or mechanically clean is
`weaker`.
