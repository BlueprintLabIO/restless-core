More agents can look like more capacity. Inside a shared outcome they can instead create more things
to explain, synchronise, combine and check. The question is not whether a task can be divided. The
question is whether another mind can return something the first mind could not obtain as well on its
own.

## What we expected

The early intuition was familiar: if a game, a research report or a marketing pack has many parts,
more autonomous workers should improve it. That intuition confuses visible parts with accountable
boundaries. A split only helps when the new owner has a stable responsibility and can return a useful
artifact, expertise or independent challenge.

## What happened

In the EXP-01 G-WORLD screen, a natural lead with one Staff contribution was ranked above both a
fresh solo arm and a forced handoff arm in a topology-blind review. The lead had found a real scene
module seam and the Staff contribution gave it room to integrate and repair the whole experience.
The result was slower and more expensive than the fresh solo arm. It was a quality result, not a
throughput result.

The same programme also caught a result that was easy to narrate and false. In the first natural
run, the lead described a Staff contribution, but the record showed no Work, Attempt or Staff turn.
The artifact could still be good. It was solo work. The sparse factual record was what made that
distinction possible without turning collaboration into a scripted ritual.

EXP-02 then tested several proposed ways to make collaboration easier. Most failed to repay their
context or review cost. EXP-03 found one bounded marketing case where sequential specialist roles
improved truth discipline, but the same kind of split lost on sales and research when a final
assembler had to reread and rewrite everything.

## What changed

Restless starts one coherent outcome with a non-producing lead and one end-to-end worker. Another
worker needs a named reason: a differentiated skill, a source region, an independent critic role, a
locally closing unit or a real capacity window.

That is a default for judgement, not a routing rule. A strong lead may choose a specialist pair when
the seam is real. It should avoid a second actor when the proposed boundary only moves the same
context through another session.

## Where the conclusion stops

The evidence came from controlled work, one model family and a small number of outcome shapes. It
does not establish a universal team size, the best organisation for every model or a claim that solo
agents always win. The next useful evidence is a new work shape with an accountable seam that can
be judged independently.
