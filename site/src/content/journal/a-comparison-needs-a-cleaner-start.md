The first Restless-versus-Codex public-site comparison asked a good question in a bad frame. Both
teams started from the same mature incumbent. Both inherited the same visual system, routes and a
large amount of content. The comparison could measure repair, integration and execution. It could
not cleanly measure independent creative direction.

## What happened

Both final candidates passed the objective browser matrix. Blind reviewers split. Mean scores differed
by less than half a point. Codex closed materially faster. Restless produced a richer interactive
demonstration and needed fewer repair loops. Neither result entitled us to say one organisation makes
better public sites.

The conclusion was inconclusive, rather than a draw converted into a marketing claim. The shared
starting point had already supplied too much of the visual answer.

## What changed

The next experiment starts greenfield, with the same frozen brief, product truth and evidence pack for
both arms. Reviewers receive only the rendered sites and a common browser-quality matrix. Arm identity,
topology, process and timing remain sealed until the owner makes a blind decision.

The greenfield checkpoint has now staged two candidates with route, mobile, focus and reduced-motion
checks. The owner decision remains pending. This publication records the method and the reason for the
new comparison. It does not reveal a winner or infer one from a source branch.

## Where the conclusion stops

One website comparison cannot establish that any organisation is generally better at design. The next
decision is deliberately human: inspect the native candidates, lock the blind judgement, then unseal
the process evidence. If the result is close again, the right outcome may still be an unresolved
question.
